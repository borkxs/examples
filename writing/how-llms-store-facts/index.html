How LLMs store facts
in a matrix of floats

An interactive walk from bigram tables to transformer attention

There is a question underneath a lot of confusion about language models: where does the knowledge actually live? Not philosophically - physically. Which bytes, in what structure, encode that Paris is the capital of France?

The answer is surprising. There is no lookup table, no key-value store, no Wikipedia article cached anywhere. The fact is a weight - a float that is slightly higher than its neighbors inside a giant matrix. The best way to understand this is to build the smallest possible version from scratch and watch it happen.

The bigram matrix

A bigram model is the XOR of language modeling: the smallest case that demonstrates the essential mechanism. Context goes in, prediction comes out. One matrix multiply.

It has a vocabulary of tokens. The model is a single matrix W of shape vocab x vocab. Row i holds the logits for "what comes after token i." That is the entire model.

Why attention exists

The bigram's failure is precise: when the model is at token is, it cannot see france two positions back. The binding is lost in transit.

A transformer's attention mechanism is the direct solution to this. At every position, it computes a weighted average over all previous tokens - so when generating the token after is, the model can look back and see that france appeared earlier. The Q/K/V lookup is:

Q (query): "I'm at is - what am I looking for?"
K (key): "What does france offer? Country-ness."
V (value): "Given that it is france, attend to: paris / franc / french."

The bigram failure is the motivation for attention, made concrete. Every architectural innovation from RNNs to transformers is answering the same question this matrix cannot: how do I encode richer context than one token?

The fact as geometry

In a real language model, the "france -> paris" fact is not a single cell. It is a direction in embedding space - a vector offset that encodes "capital city of" as a relationship. The famous example: king - man + woman ~= queen.

Meng et al. (ROME, 2022) located the specific weights responsible for facts like "Eiffel Tower -> Paris" in GPT-J and surgically edited them to say "Eiffel Tower -> Rome." The fact-as-float, made literal at scale.
